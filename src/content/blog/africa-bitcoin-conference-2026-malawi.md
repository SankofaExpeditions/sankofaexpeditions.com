For four years, the Africa Bitcoin Conference called Accra home. The Kempinski, the heat, the hustle of a West African capital stacking sats and swapping ideas about sound money. That was the foundation.

For 2026, ABC is picking up and moving somewhere new. Somewhere that tells a different story about where Bitcoin adoption in Africa is actually heading.

Blantyre, Malawi. December 2-4, 2026. The 5th Annual Africa Bitcoin Conference (#ABC26).

This is not a footnote. This is the whole point.

---

## What the Africa Bitcoin Conference Actually Is

Before the hype, some context.

The Africa Bitcoin Conference is not a crypto conference. That distinction matters enormously. There are no NFT panels. No altcoin pitches. No "blockchain technology" keynotes where someone explains distributed ledgers to a room that already knows.

ABC is a Bitcoin conference, full stop. By Africans, for Africans, focused on Bitcoin as a practical tool for financial sovereignty on a continent where most people have never had meaningful access to a bank, a stable currency, or a reliable way to send money across borders without getting eaten by fees.

The conference was founded in 2022 by Farida Nabourema, a Togolese human rights activist who understands firsthand what monetary repression looks like. She didn't come to Bitcoin as a speculator. She came as someone who watched her government weaponize the financial system against citizens. That origin story shapes everything about how ABC is run.

The inaugural 2022 edition was held at the Kempinski Hotel in Accra, Ghana. It drew builders, educators, and advocates from across the continent and diaspora. The 2nd, 3rd, and 4th editions stayed in Accra, building the community year over year. The ecosystem that gathered around ABC became one of the most serious concentrations of African Bitcoin talent on the planet.

The tagline for 2026 is ACCESS. LEARN. CONNECT.

That's not marketing. That's the actual agenda.

## Why Malawi, Why Now

Malawi ranks among Africa's poorest nations by GDP per capita. That's the first thing most articles lead with when they mention the country. We're going to give you the more useful frame.

Malawi has one of the highest mobile money penetration rates on the continent. The majority of economic activity that happens outside of cash runs through mobile phones. Many of those phones are feature phones, not smartphones. No app store. No data plan. Just SMS and USSD codes.

That matters for Bitcoin because of Machankura.

Machankura is a USSD-based Bitcoin Lightning wallet developed by South African developer Kgothatso Ngako. It works on feature phones with no internet connection. You dial a code, navigate a simple menu, and you can receive Lightning payments, send sats, and interact with the Bitcoin network. It's live in around ten African countries, including countries in the Southern and Eastern Africa region that look a lot like Malawi.

This is not theoretical adoption. This is Bitcoin working for someone who has never owned a smartphone, in a country where the banking system has historically left rural communities behind. Malawi is exactly the kind of place where this matters most.

Then there's Gridless.

Gridless is a Bitcoin mining company co-founded by Erik Hersman, one of the minds behind Ushahidi, and Philip Walton. Their model is to co-locate Bitcoin mining with small-scale renewable energy infrastructure in rural Africa: micro-hydro, solar, and similar sources that generate more electricity than a local community can use. The mining operation buys that excess power, makes the energy project economically viable, and extends electricity access in the process. Gridless operates across East and Southern Africa, including in Malawi.

Read that again. Bitcoin mining is making rural electrification economically viable in Malawi.

That's the story ABC 2026 is walking into. This is not Ghana's well-established Bitcoin community getting another year in the sun. This is ABC deliberately planting its flag in a place where the tools are early, the need is real, and the community is hungry to connect with the broader ecosystem.

## Who's on the Stage

ABC 2026 has assembled a speaker list that reflects exactly what the conference is about: builders working on real problems for real people across the continent.

A few to watch:

**Innocencia Ndembera** represents Women in Tech Malawi. She's a local voice on the local stage, which is deliberate. ABC has a history of platforming builders from the host country rather than flying in outsiders to explain things to Africans. Ndembera brings the Malawi perspective from inside Malawi.

**Grant Gombwa** runs Bitcoin Boma and Bitmsika, two Malawian Bitcoin projects. Another host-country voice. If you want to understand what Bitcoin looks like on the ground in Blantyre right now, this is the person to find.

**Kondwani Konyani** of Open Study Hubs is the third Malawian speaker. Three local voices at a conference in your city is not an accident. ABC is building a local ecosystem, not just visiting it.

**Megasley** is behind Africa Free Routing, working specifically on Lightning Network routing expansion for the African continent. Lightning works best when there are well-connected nodes to route payments through. Africa Free Routing is building that infrastructure. This is the technical layer that makes Machankura and every other Lightning tool more reliable across Africa.

**Chanda Chewe** brings the Zambia perspective via Bit2Kwacha, a Zambian Bitcoin platform. Southern Africa representation beyond Malawi itself.

**Sabina Waithira Gitau** of Tando LTD connects East Africa's Bitcoin ecosystem, anchored in Kenya, where the Lightning adoption curve has been among the steepest on the continent.

The full lineup includes builders from Nigeria, Rwanda, South Africa, Ghana, the UK (Bitcoin policy via Susie Violet Ward), and Latin America (Eréndira Ramos from Equitable Global Futures, adding a diaspora-of-the-global-south angle that fits ABC's broader vision). The common thread is not geography: it's that every person on this stage is building something functional, not speculating.

## Ecosystem Moving Alongside ABC

ABC doesn't happen in a vacuum. The broader African Bitcoin ecosystem showing up around and alongside it is worth understanding before you book your flight.

**Bitnob** is a Nigerian Lightning payments platform that has processed over $4.6 billion in transactions. CEO Bernard Parah has been a consistent presence in the African Bitcoin space. Bitnob represents the payment rail layer: how money actually moves between people on the continent using Lightning.

**Bitcoin Dada** is a Kenyan women's Bitcoin education organization. Self-custody education for women who have been systematically excluded from financial systems. The overlap with ABC's ACCESS ethos is direct.

**Africa Free Routing** (Megasley speaking at ABC 2026) is specifically attacking the routing problem: Lightning Network payments need well-connected nodes to pass through. Thin routing infrastructure means higher failure rates and less reliable payments. Building out routing nodes across Africa makes every Lightning tool more reliable for everyone.

The theme across all of this: Bitcoin in Africa is not about price speculation. It's about tools that work for people who need them. Sound money, self-custody, and censorship resistance for people who have concrete, immediate reasons to care about all three.

## Three Ticket Tiers, Bitcoin Accepted

Tickets for ABC 2026 are available at [afrobitcoin.org/tickets](https://afrobitcoin.org/tickets/).

There are three tiers:

**ABC Pass** covers general admission: main stage sessions, the exhibition floor, and networking. This is the base conference experience.

**Satoshi Pass** is the premium tier: everything in the ABC Pass plus access to the VIP lounge, priority seating, and exclusive networking sessions. If you're going to ABC to make specific connections, this is worth considering.

**Nakamoto Pass** is the highest tier, with the full VIP experience.

All tiers accept both Bitcoin and fiat. We won't list specific prices here because pricing may update closer to the event and the official site is the authoritative source. Check afrobitcoin.org for current figures.

One note on logistics: Blantyre is Malawi's commercial capital and second-largest city. It is not Lilongwe, the political capital, which is the more common entry point for international flights. Build in a domestic transfer or ground transport leg when you're planning your trip. Visas for Malawi vary by passport. Check the Malawi Department of Immigration before you finalize anything.

## The Bigger Picture for Diaspora

Here's the angle that matters most for Sankofa readers.

If you're part of the African diaspora and you're a Bitcoiner, ABC is your tribe. The people in that room are not talking about whether Bitcoin will go up. They're talking about how to get a farmer in rural Malawi paid via Lightning on a feature phone. They're talking about how to explain self-custody to a first-time user in Kigali or Lagos or Nairobi. They're debating Bitcoin policy in regulatory environments that range from cautious to hostile.

If you're thinking about relocating to Africa, and you're someone who stacks sats and believes in sound money, understanding the Bitcoin ecosystem in your target country is part of due diligence. The difference between a country with an active Lightning network, circular economy merchants, and a local community of educators versus a country where Bitcoin is known but isolated can shape your day-to-day experience significantly.

Malawi specifically: a country with real Bitcoin infrastructure being built, a conference coming that will accelerate local community formation, and Lightning tools already adapted for the market. That is a different ground truth than it was two years ago.

The ABC conference is a measuring stick. Watch which countries show up with strong local delegation. Watch who the speakers are and what they're building. That's the map of where Bitcoin adoption in Africa is real and moving, not just talked about.
